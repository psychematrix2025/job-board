2:T29d9,<p>Remote work promised to decouple pay from place. In 2026, the reality is more complicated: <em>where you live still shapes what you earn</em> at most companies — just through formal systems instead of office proximity. If you are applying from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, or elsewhere in South Asia, understanding how remote pay is set is the difference between accepting a lowball offer and negotiating a life-changing one.</p>
<p>This guide explains the three pay models remote companies use, gives realistic 2026 ranges by role, and lays out how to negotiate as a South Asia-based applicant. Figures are drawn from published company pay bands and current industry surveys; they are ranges for orientation, and we flag where the data is thinner.</p>
<h2>How remote salaries are actually set: three models</h2>
<p>When a company hires remotely, it has to decide <em>which market</em> to price your role against. That decision is made before you ever see the job posting, and it produces three distinct models:</p>
<p><strong>1. Single national rate.</strong> One salary band for the role, regardless of where the employee lives. These postings tend to have narrower ranges (e.g., &quot;$70,000–$85,000&quot;) because geography has been removed as a variable. Every hire at that level lands somewhere inside the band whether they sit in San Francisco or Lahore.</p>
<p><strong>2. Location-adjusted (geo-banded).</strong> The most common approach — used by an estimated 71% of employers with workers in multiple locations. The company prices you against your local market using tier multipliers. Posted ranges are wide (e.g., &quot;$50,000–$120,000&quot;) because they must stretch from the highest-cost to the lowest-cost market in one listing. Two people doing identical work can land 30% apart based purely on where they live.</p>
<p><strong>3. Flat global rate.</strong> One band worldwide, set at a competitive international level. The rarest model, and the best deal for South Asia-based applicants.</p>
<h3>What real companies do</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>GitLab</strong> (fully distributed): publishes bands by location tier. Senior engineers in Tier 1 markets (US, Canada, UK) earn $155,000–$215,000 base plus equity; engineers in lower-cost countries earn proportionally less via a location factor.</li>
<li><strong>Automattic</strong> (WordPress.com, Tumblr): flat global philosophy — senior engineers earn roughly $130,000–$180,000 regardless of location. A South Asia-based engineer gets the same base as a US-based one.</li>
<li><strong>Basecamp:</strong> pays top-of-market San Francisco rates regardless of location as a deliberate talent strategy — senior engineers $175,000–$210,000+.</li>
<li><strong>Stripe:</strong> location-adjusted — senior engineers in high-cost markets earn $200,000–$280,000; in lower-cost regions $160,000–$200,000.</li>
<li><strong>Shopify:</strong> fully remote since 2020; senior US engineers $160,000–$230,000, with Canadian equivalents paid proportionally in CAD.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>How to read a job posting:</strong> a narrow range usually signals a national or global rate (good for you — the number is the number). A very wide range usually signals geo-banding (your offer will depend on your location tier — ask which tier your country falls into <em>before</em> you invest hours in interviews).</p>

Dedicated full-time remote developers hired through agencies in India run about $2,500–$3,500/month — useful context if a &quot;full-time remote&quot; offer comes in suspiciously close to local agency rates.</p>
<h2>The South Asia applicant&#39;s reality</h2>
<p>Let&#39;s be direct about the dynamics:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>You are competing on value, not just price.</strong> The era when &quot;cheap offshore developer&quot; was the pitch is ending; AI coding tools have compressed the bottom of the market. What wins remote roles now is demonstrable output: a portfolio, open-source contributions, shipped products, measurable results.</li>
<li><strong>Time zones are a genuine asset.</strong> South Asia&#39;s evening working hours overlap with the US East Coast morning (7–11 PM PKT = 9 AM–1 PM ET). US companies that want extended coverage or follow-the-sun handoffs value this — say so explicitly in applications.</li>
<li><strong>English proficiency is a pay lever.</strong> Strong written English — the primary medium of remote work — measurably widens your options and your band. It is the highest-ROI skill investment for remote applicants.</li>
<li><strong>Contractor vs employee matters.</strong> Many companies hire South Asia-based workers as contractors via platforms like Deel or Remote (employer-of-record services) because they have no local entity. Contractors typically earn more in cash but get no benefits, no paid leave, and no job security — price that in. A $40,000 contractor role with zero benefits can be worse than a $34,000 employee role with leave, insurance, and equipment.</li>
<li><strong>Payment and tax are your problem to solve.</strong> International transfers (Wise, Payoneer, direct SWIFT) each take their cut; understand your home country&#39;s tax treatment of foreign income <em>before</em> you sign. In Pakistan, for example, IT/ITES exporters face specific withholding regimes — get local tax advice rather than guessing.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to negotiate as a South Asia-based applicant</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Find out the pay model first.</strong> Ask: &quot;Is this role on a global band or location-adjusted?&quot; and &quot;Which location tier would I be in?&quot; Ask early — before the final interview round, not after the offer.</li>
<li><strong>Anchor to the role&#39;s value, not your location.</strong> Research what the role pays at the company&#39;s headquarters (levels.fyi, Glassdoor, published bands). If they use geo-banding, negotiate <em>within your tier</em> — ask for the top of the tier, citing competing offers or market data.</li>
<li><strong>Target flat-global companies deliberately.</strong> Automattic-style employers are a short list, but they exist — prioritize them in your job search. One flat-global offer beats three geo-banded ones.</li>
<li><strong>Negotiate the whole package:</strong> equipment stipend, coworking allowance, learning budget, paid time off (contractors: negotiate paid leave into the rate), and review cycles with raise triggers. Remote workers often leave money in these line items.</li>
<li><strong>Get competing offers.</strong> Nothing moves a geo-banded offer like a second offer at a higher tier. Interview in parallel, not in sequence.</li>
<li><strong>Beware the &quot;we&#39;ll adjust when you relocate&quot; trap.</strong> Some companies promise a raise if you move to a higher-cost country later. Get any such commitment in writing with specific numbers and triggers — verbal promises evaporate.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Red flags in remote job postings</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>No salary range and evasiveness when asked.</strong> Legitimate remote-first companies usually publish bands. Evasion is a signal.</li>
<li><strong>&quot;Unpaid trial&quot; work</strong> beyond a small, time-boxed task. Real employers pay for trial projects or keep assessments short.</li>
<li><strong>Upfront fees</strong> for equipment, training, or &quot;placement.&quot; Legitimate US employers never charge you to hire you — see our US job scam guide for the full checklist.</li>
<li><strong>Contractor misclassification at employee hours.</strong> If they control your schedule, require exclusivity, and forbid other clients, you are functionally an employee — and should be paid and protected like one.</li>
<li><strong>Vague equity promises</strong> from early startups in lieu of salary. Equity in a pre-revenue startup is a lottery ticket; discount it to near zero in your calculations.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Next steps</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Identify each target company&#39;s pay model</strong> before applying — check their careers page and engineering blog for published bands; GitLab-style transparency is a green flag.</li>
<li><strong>Benchmark your role</strong> on levels.fyi and Glassdoor for the company&#39;s home market, then set your ask at the top of your realistic tier — not the middle.</li>
<li><strong>Build proof, not just a CV:</strong> shipped projects, a portfolio site, open-source contributions, and quantified results matter more for remote hiring than credentials.</li>
<li><strong>Browse remote-first listings</strong> on our job board, filter for US companies that publish salary bands, and prioritize flat-global employers in your applications.</li>
</ol>
