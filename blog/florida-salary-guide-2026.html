Florida Salary Guide 2026: Minimum Wage, Pay by Role, and Cost of Living

By Editorial Team2026-10-19Salary Guidesusa

Florida is America's growth state: no income tax, year-round warm weather, and a job market that has been adding employers and workers faster than almost anywhere else. Miami has become a genuine finance and tech hub, Orlando runs on tourism and a growing simulation-tech sector, and the Space Coast anchors aerospace. Salaries run below coastal peaks, but the tax picture and (relatively) moderate housing costs change the math. This guide covers the 2026 numbers — including a minimum-wage increase that lands mid-year.

Minimum wage: $15 arrives September 30, 2026

Florida's minimum wage follows a voter-approved schedule (Amendment 2, 2020) that raises the floor $1 per year until it hits $15:

  • $14.00 per hour through September 29, 2026.
  • $15.00 per hour from September 30, 2026 — the final scheduled step.
  • From 2027, the rate adjusts annually for inflation instead.

Details that matter:

  • Tipped workers: the cash wage moves from $10.98 to $11.98 per hour on September 30, 2026, against a fixed $3.02 tip credit. Tips plus cash wage must total at least the full minimum — the employer makes up any shortfall.
  • No local variation. Florida law preempts local minimum wages, so one rate applies statewide — Miami pays the same legal floor as Tallahassee.
  • No state overtime law — the federal 1.5x-after-40-hours rule applies.
  • No state paid-sick-leave law, and cities are barred from creating one.
  • E-Verify is mandatory for employers with 25 or more workers — relevant if you are applying from abroad, since work authorization is checked systematically.

Major industries and where the jobs are

  • Tourism and hospitality — Orlando, Miami, Tampa. The state's economic engine: theme parks, hotels, cruise lines (Miami is the cruise capital of the world), restaurants. Huge volume of jobs, but pay clusters near the wage floor outside management.
  • Healthcare — statewide, especially Tampa, Jacksonville, Miami. Florida's large retiree population drives enormous healthcare demand — hospitals, home health, senior care. One of the best-paying non-tech lanes in the state.
  • Finance — Miami. The "Wall Street South" migration is real: hedge funds, private equity, and crypto firms have opened major Miami offices, bringing high-paying finance jobs with them.
  • Aerospace and defense — the Space Coast (Brevard County), plus Tampa and Jacksonville. SpaceX, Blue Origin, and legacy contractors; engineering pay here competes nationally.
  • Tech — Miami, Tampa, Orlando. Growing fast from a small base — fintech in Miami, defense-tech in Orlando's simulation corridor, health-tech in Tampa.
  • Logistics and trade — Jacksonville, Miami, Tampa. Major ports and Latin America trade links drive supply-chain and operations roles.
  • Agriculture — central and south Florida. Citrus, sugar, winter vegetables; plus a growing ag-tech layer.

Context: Florida's average annual pay across all covered jobs was about $72,770 in 2025 (BLS) — roughly 92% of the US average, and rising faster than the national rate over the past decade. The state is catching up, not standing still.

The tax picture: no income tax

Florida charges no state income tax, and the ban is in the state constitution. For a professional earning $110,000, that is roughly $5,000–$7,000 a year kept versus a mid-tax state.

  • Sales tax is 6% state plus local add-ons (typically 7–7.5%).
  • Property tax rates are moderate, but insurance is the hidden cost (see below).
  • Tourism taxes (hotel, rental car) fund a lot of local government — visitors subsidize residents, in effect.

Cost of living: moderate housing, expensive insurance

Florida's typical home value sits around $375,000 (Zillow, January 2026) — just above the national typical value of about $361,000, and roughly half of California's. Metro detail:

  • Miami: the priciest market — strong demand from domestic migrants and international buyers keeps prices and rents elevated; one-bedrooms in desirable areas run $2,200–$3,200.
  • Orlando: mid-range — tourism jobs pay less but housing is reasonable; typical values near the state median.
  • Tampa: fast-growing, increasingly popular with relocators; good value relative to job growth.
  • Jacksonville: the most affordable major metro — solid employers, reasonable housing.

The Florida asterisk is insurance. Homeowners insurance has become very expensive and hard to get in many coastal areas due to hurricane risk — annual premiums in the thousands are normal, and flood insurance is a separate policy. Renters should check whether their building is in a flood zone. Also budget for air conditioning (year-round), hurricane preparedness, and higher car insurance rates.

Negotiating in Florida's market

Florida's lack of a state pay-transparency law means salary ranges are often missing from postings — unlike California, New York, or Washington, employers here are not required to publish bands. That puts the research burden on you: check levels.fyi and Glassdoor for the role and metro, and ask for the budgeted range early in the process ("What's the approved range for this position?"). Because Florida's average pay runs about 8% below the national average, anchoring your ask to national data without adjusting for the local market can price you out — but the no-income-tax advantage means you should compare on take-home pay, not headline salary. For hourly roles near the wage floor, remember the September 30, 2026 step to $15.00 and confirm in writing that your rate reflects it.

Who thrives in Florida

  • Healthcare workers — retiree-driven demand is structural and growing.
  • Finance professionals — Miami's finance scene pays well with no state income tax.
  • Aerospace and defense engineers — Space Coast pay competes nationally.
  • Hospitality managers — the industry's biggest market, with real career ladders.
  • Remote workers — keep your salary, pay no state income tax, live somewhere warm.

Who should think twice

  • Teachers — pay is low relative to the national average and has been a persistent political fight.
  • Anyone buying on the coast without pricing insurance — get real quotes before making an offer; insurance can erase the affordability advantage.
  • Workers who need strong labor protections — Florida is at-will with minimal state-level mandates beyond federal law.

Next steps

  1. If you are hired near the wage floor, note the September 30, 2026 step to $15.00 — confirm your employer has applied it.
  2. Compare Miami finance/tech offers against New York equivalents on an after-tax basis — the no-income-tax math often closes the headline gap.
  3. Price homeowners and flood insurance before buying anywhere near the coast.
  4. Applying from abroad? Read our H-1B guide — and note Florida's mandatory E-Verify: your work authorization must be airtight.
