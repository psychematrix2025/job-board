2:T2519,<p>American hiring managers spend seconds on a first scan, and applicant tracking systems filter many resumes before a human ever sees them. The US resume follows strict, unwritten conventions — and foreign applicants violate them constantly without realizing it. A photo, a date of birth, a three-page career history, or the word &quot;CV&quot; at the top can mark you as an outsider before your qualifications get read. This guide covers exactly what a US resume looks like in 2026, section by section, plus the mistakes to kill.</p>
<h2>Resume, not CV — and why it matters</h2>
<p>In the US, &quot;CV&quot; means something specific: a long academic document (often 5+ pages) used by professors, researchers, and doctors. For every other job, the document is called a <strong>resume</strong> — one to two pages, achievement-focused, tailored per application. Sending a &quot;CV&quot; for a marketing or engineering role signals you do not know the market. Name the file accordingly: <code>Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf</code>.</p>
<h2>The non-negotiable rules</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Length: one page</strong> for under 10 years of experience; <strong>two pages maximum</strong> for senior professionals. Never three. Cut ruthlessly — your resume is a highlight reel, not an autobiography.</li>
<li><strong>No photo.</strong> Ever. US employers avoid photos to stay clear of discrimination liability; including one looks unprofessional and can get your resume discarded.</li>
<li><strong>No personal details.</strong> No date of birth, no age, no marital status, no gender, no religion, no national ID number, no passport number. None of these belong on a US resume.</li>
<li><strong>No &quot;References available upon request.&quot;</strong> Everyone knows references are available on request. The line wastes space and dates you.</li>
<li><strong>Contact info:</strong> name, city and state (or &quot;Remote&quot;), phone number with country code if abroad, professional email, LinkedIn URL. No full street address — city is enough and safer.</li>
<li><strong>American English spelling</strong> throughout: organized, specialized, center, traveled.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The standard structure, section by section</h2>
<p><strong>1. Header.</strong> Your name in a larger font, then one line: city/state, phone, email, LinkedIn. If you have a portfolio or GitHub relevant to the role, add it here.</p>
<p><strong>2. Professional summary (3–4 lines, not an objective).</strong> Objectives (&quot;Seeking a challenging position where I can grow...&quot;) are obsolete — they describe what you want, and employers care about what you offer. A summary states your positioning:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Senior data engineer with 6 years building real-time pipelines processing 50M+ events daily. Python, Spark, AWS. Led migration that cut infrastructure costs 32%. Open to US relocation.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Three sentences: who you are, your quantified proof, your target. Write it last, after the bullets.</p>
<p><strong>3. Skills (keyword-rich, scannable).</strong> A compact section listing the tools, technologies, and competencies from the job description that you genuinely have. This section exists largely for the ATS — applicant tracking systems match these keywords against the posting. Mirror the posting&#39;s exact phrasing (&quot;project management,&quot; not &quot;managing projects&quot;). Group logically: <em>Languages: Python, SQL, Java | Cloud: AWS (EC2, S3, Lambda) | Tools: Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform.</em></p>
<p><strong>4. Professional experience (reverse chronological).</strong> This is 70% of your resume. Format each role:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Senior Data Engineer</strong> | Company Name, City, Country | Mar 2021 – Present</p>
<ul>
<li>Bullet</li>
<li>Bullet</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<p><strong>Bullets are achievements, not duties.</strong> &quot;Responsible for managing the database&quot; describes a duty — every applicant has duties. Achievements follow the formula <strong>action verb + what you did + quantified result</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Bad: &quot;Responsible for customer support tickets.&quot;</li>
<li>Good: &quot;Resolved 400+ support tickets monthly, cutting average resolution time from 72 to 28 hours and lifting CSAT from 3.8 to 4.6.&quot;</li>
<li>Bad: &quot;Worked on marketing campaigns.&quot;</li>
<li>Good: &quot;Launched 12 email campaigns to a 200K-subscriber list, driving a 34% lift in trial signups quarter over quarter.&quot;</li>
</ul>
<p>Start every bullet with a strong action verb: led, built, launched, designed, reduced, automated, negotiated, grew. Never &quot;responsible for,&quot; &quot;helped with,&quot; or &quot;worked on.&quot; Aim for 3–5 bullets per recent role, fewer for older ones. Every bullet should make the reader think: <em>this person produces results.</em></p>
<p><strong>5. Education.</strong> Degree, institution, city/country, graduation year. For foreign degrees, add the US equivalent if you have a credential evaluation: <em>BSc Computer Science, University of the Punjab, Lahore, Pakistan (evaluated as equivalent to a US bachelor&#39;s degree, WES 2025).</em> GPA only if it is strong (3.5+) and you graduated recently. Drop it after a few years of experience.</p>
<p><strong>6. Certifications and extras (selective).</strong> Include certifications that matter for the role (AWS Solutions Architect, PMP, CPA). Languages belong here if relevant. Publications, patents, and major open-source contributions go here too. Skip hobbies unless they are genuinely relevant.</p>
<h2>Formatting for the ATS (the machine reads first)</h2>
<p>Most mid-to-large US employers run your resume through an applicant tracking system before a human sees it. The formatting rules are strict:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Single column, top to bottom.</strong> Multi-column layouts scramble in parsing.</li>
<li><strong>Standard headings:</strong> &quot;Professional Experience,&quot; &quot;Education,&quot; &quot;Skills.&quot; Not &quot;My Journey&quot; or &quot;Career Highlights.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>No tables, text boxes, graphics, or icons.</strong> They vanish or scramble.</li>
<li><strong>Standard fonts</strong> — Arial, Calibri, Georgia. Real text, never an image of text.</li>
<li><strong>PDF or .docx as the posting requests.</strong> Never .pages or image files.</li>
<li><strong>The plain-text test:</strong> copy your resume into a basic text editor. If your name, titles, dates, and contact details do not appear cleanly in order, the ATS will not read them either.</li>
</ul>
<p>Read our ATS resume guide for the full breakdown of how these systems score and filter.</p>
<h2>The foreign-applicant mistakes to kill</h2>
<p>These are the errors we see most from applicants outside the US:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The photo and personal-details block.</strong> Standard in many countries, disqualifying in the US. Remove all of it.</li>
<li><strong>The 4-page career history.</strong> US resumes are achievement summaries. Condense ruthlessly — older roles get one line each.</li>
<li><strong>Duty-listing instead of quantifying.</strong> &quot;Responsible for X&quot; bullets read as junior everywhere in the US market. Quantify everything you can.</li>
<li><strong>The wrong vocabulary.</strong> &quot;CV&quot; instead of &quot;resume,&quot; &quot;marks&quot; instead of &quot;GPA,&quot; &quot;intermediate&quot; as an education level — translate your background into terms US recruiters recognize.</li>
<li><strong>Hiding the logistics.</strong> If you need sponsorship, say so plainly in your cover letter or summary line (&quot;Requires H-1B sponsorship&quot; or &quot;Authorized to work in the US&quot; if true). Recruiters would rather know upfront than discover it in round three.</li>
<li><strong>One generic resume for everything.</strong> US hiring expects tailoring: mirror each posting&#39;s keywords in your skills section and reorder bullets to lead with the most relevant achievements. Keep a master resume; produce a tailored version per application.</li>
</ol>
<h2>The one-page test</h2>
<p>Before you send any resume to a US employer, run this check:</p>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> One page (two max for 10+ years), no photo, no personal details</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Summary states positioning with a quantified proof point</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Every experience bullet starts with an action verb and most carry numbers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Skills section mirrors the target posting&#39;s keyword phrasing</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Foreign degree translated to US equivalent terms</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Plain-text test passes — everything extracts cleanly</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> File named <code>Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf</code>, American spelling throughout</li>
</ul>
<h2>Next steps</h2>
<ol>
<li>Rebuild your resume to this structure today — do not patch the old format, start fresh.</li>
<li>Run the plain-text test and fix every extraction error before your next application.</li>
<li>Get a WES or ECE credential evaluation if you will be applying to US employers or universities — it removes a common verification friction.</li>
<li>Pair the resume with a tailored cover letter for every application — our cover letter guide covers the 3-paragraph structure US recruiters expect.</li>
</ol>
