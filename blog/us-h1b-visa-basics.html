US H-1B Visa Basics 2026: The Lottery, the Cap, and the Sponsorship Reality

By Editorial Team2026-10-18Visa Guidesusa

The H-1B is the main US work visa for foreign professionals in "specialty occupations" — roles that require at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field. Tech, engineering, finance and healthcare dominate the numbers. But the H-1B is also the most competitive and most misunderstood work visa in the world.

The rules have shifted significantly since 2025. This guide reflects the situation as of October 2026 — including several items that are still in legal limbo, which we flag clearly.

The core reality: you cannot apply for yourself

The single most important fact about the H-1B:

  • A US employer must petition for you. The employer registers you, files the paperwork and pays the fees. There is no self-application route.
  • The visa is tied to that employer and that job. Changing employers requires a new petition.
  • If you are outside the US, you need the petition approved before you can apply for the visa at a consulate.

Everything else in this guide flows from that fact: your first task is finding a US employer willing to sponsor you, and sponsorship is expensive enough that employers are selective.

The cap and the lottery

Each fiscal year, USCIS makes available 85,000 new cap-subject H-1B visas:

  • 65,000 in the general pool.
  • 20,000 reserved for beneficiaries with a US master's degree or higher (the "advanced degree exemption").

Certain employers are cap-exempt and can sponsor year-round without the lottery: universities, non-profit research organisations and government research bodies. Workers transferring an existing H-1B to a new employer also generally bypass the cap.

The registration process

  1. Registration (March): the employer registers you electronically during the annual window (for FY 2027, the window ran 4–19 March 2026) and pays the $215 registration fee per beneficiary. The fee is non-refundable, win or lose.
  2. Selection: USCIS runs the draw and notifies employers, usually by the end of March.
  3. Petition (from 1 April): selected employers file Form I-129 with a certified Labor Condition Application (LCA), for employment starting 1 October (the start of the US fiscal year).
  4. Visa application: if you are abroad, you then apply for the H-1B visa at a US consulate (visa fee around $205).

The weighted lottery (new since February 2026)

Since 27 February 2026, the draw is no longer random. It is weighted by the offered wage level:

  • Level IV (highest wage): 4 entries in the pool.
  • Level III: 3 entries.
  • Level II: 2 entries.
  • Level I (entry wage): 1 entry.

The practical effect is stark: in the FY 2027 cycle, roughly 46% of selections went to the two top wage levels versus about 18% to the lowest, and two-thirds of registrants held at least a US master's degree. Higher offered wages and a US master's degree now materially move your odds. The overall selection rate for the FY 2026 cycle was around 25–26% — roughly one in four.

Specialty occupation and the LCA

The job must qualify as a specialty occupation — generally requiring a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific specialty. The definition was tightened in recent rulemaking, so roles with vague degree requirements face more scrutiny.

Before filing, the employer must obtain a certified Labor Condition Application from the Department of Labor, attesting it will pay you at least the prevailing wage for the role and location, and that hiring you will not harm US workers' conditions.

Costs in 2026

H-1B costs are borne by the employer (charging them to the worker is restricted), but they shape which employers sponsor — so you need to understand them:

  • Registration: $215 per beneficiary.
  • Petition filing (Form I-129) plus related fees: roughly $1,900 for a small employer to $7,000+ for a large one, before legal costs. The exact total depends on employer size and type (including the Asylum Program Fee).
  • Premium processing (optional): $2,965 for a decision in about 15 business days (raised from $2,805 on 1 March 2026).

The $100,000 question — read this carefully

In September 2025, a presidential proclamation created a $100,000 payment on certain new H-1B petitions for workers outside the US. Here is where it stands in October 2026:

  • A federal court struck down the payment in June 2026; it is not currently being collected.
  • The proclamation itself was extended in September 2026 to run through September 2027, but the court ruling still blocks collection and the appeal is unresolved.
  • Separately, a DHS proposed rule (August 2026) would charge $103,265 at filing for all cap-subject petitions. Public comments closed in September 2026; as of mid-October 2026 there is no final rule and no start date.

Bottom line: the mega-fee is not in force today, but the legal situation is genuinely unsettled. If an employer cites it as a reason not to sponsor, they are reacting to real uncertainty — ask whether they would reconsider for cap-exempt or alternative routes.

Duration and the green card path

  • The H-1B is granted for up to 3 years, extendable to 6 years total.
  • Beyond 6 years, extensions are possible if your employer has started the green card process (an approved I-140 petition), under a law called AC21 — this is how many workers stay for a decade-plus while waiting in the employment-based green card queue.
  • Spouses (H-4): can only work with a separate employment authorisation document, and the automatic extension of that document while a renewal is pending ended in October 2025 — plan renewals early.

The sponsorship reality for applicants abroad

For a job seeker in Pakistan or South Asia applying directly from home, honest expectations matter:

  • Most H-1B sponsorships go to people already in the US (often on student visas with OPT work authorisation) or to workers at multinational firms transferring via the L-1 route.
  • Direct-from-abroad sponsorship happens, but concentrates in IT services/consulting firms and employers with established immigration pipelines.
  • The weighted lottery means entry-level-wage offers from abroad are now long shots. Mid-to-senior roles at higher wage levels have meaningfully better odds.
  • Never pay anyone for "H-1B sponsorship." Legitimate employers pay the costs; anyone selling you a petition is running a scam or a visa mill that can destroy your immigration record.

Alternatives worth knowing

O-1: Extraordinary Ability

For individuals with a record of extraordinary achievement in sciences, arts, education, business or athletics — evidenced by awards, publications, press coverage, judging roles or similar criteria. No lottery, no cap, and no wage weighting. The bar is high but it is a realistic route for genuinely accomplished professionals, founders and researchers.

L-1: Intra-Company Transfer

If you work for a multinational with US offices, the L-1A (managers/executives) and L-1B (specialised knowledge) let the company transfer you to the US — no lottery, no cap. Many professionals use a year at a multinational's home-country office as a deliberate stepping stone to an L-1 transfer.

Other routes

  • H-1B1 — a separate small quota for Chilean and Singaporean nationals.
  • E-3 — for Australian nationals.
  • TN — for Canadian and Mexican nationals under the USMCA.
  • J-1 — exchange visitor programmes, sometimes with a two-year home-residency requirement.
  • Cap-exempt H-1B — universities and research institutions can sponsor you any time of year with no lottery at all. For researchers and academics, this is often the smoothest path.

Next steps

  1. Assess your route honestly: direct H-1B from abroad, US study-to-work (F-1/OPT), multinational transfer (L-1), or cap-exempt/academic.
  2. Target employers with sponsorship history. USCIS publishes data on which companies file the most H-1B petitions — prioritise those names.
  3. Maximise your wage level. Under the weighted lottery, the offered wage tier directly affects selection odds — senior, specialised roles beat entry-level ones.
  4. Watch the fee litigation. The $100,000/$103,265 situation could change the economics of sponsorship; check current status before making plans around it.
  5. Keep O-1 and L-1 in view. For accomplished professionals and multinational employees respectively, they bypass the lottery entirely.

Disclaimer: US immigration policy is in unusual flux as of October 2026 — the weighted lottery is new, the fee litigation is unresolved, and rules can change by proclamation or rulemaking. Verify everything on the official USCIS website (uscis.gov) or with a licensed US immigration attorney before acting.
